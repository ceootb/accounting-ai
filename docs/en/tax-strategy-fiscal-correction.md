# Legal Tax Strategy & Fiscal Correction

A sound tax strategy does **not** start from *"what expense can we create so the tax is smaller"*, but
from **understanding why an expense becomes a positive fiscal correction**. The goal is **not to
disguise costs**, but to **design transactions that are legitimate and well-documented from the start**.

> A *positive fiscal correction* = an expense that is **recognized in the commercial report** but **must
> be corrected (added back) for tax purposes** because it can't reduce taxable income — so taxable
> income ends up higher than commercial profit.

## Why an expense becomes a positive fiscal correction

Costs that often need attention:

1. **No sufficiently clear link to business activity** — e.g. the **owner's personal expenses** charged
   to the company.
2. **Without adequate supporting evidence/documents** — invoice, receipt, contract, proof of payment,
   or other documents are incomplete.
3. **Tax-restricted or non-deductible from gross income** by the rules, even though it's already booked
   as an *expense* in accounting.
4. **Benefits-in-kind (natura) or certain benefits** given to related parties/employees — the tax
   treatment must be assessed by the **type of transaction and the applicable rules**.
5. **Recording that doesn't match the transaction's substance** — the account or accounting treatment
   differs from the real substance.
6. **Related-party transactions** — especially when the **price or terms have no defensible basis**.

## Legal tax planning — from the moment of the transaction (not disguising)

The way is **not** to disguise costs, but legal *tax planning* **from when the transaction happens**:

- first determine whether the spending is genuinely **for business purposes**;
- use the **account that matches the substance** of the transaction;
- **complete the evidence** from the start;
- **separate** company and personal spending;
- **identify early** what is *deductible* and what is likely *non-deductible*;
- for transactions with **special tax treatment**, **check the tax rules before** the transaction;
- **document the business reason** and the basis of calculation.

## The principle

> **Not** finding ways to force a genuinely *non-deductible* cost into a *deductible* one — but
> **designing** business transactions that are **legitimate, documented, and receive the appropriate tax
> treatment**.

## When fiscal correction happens

Fiscal correction usually **waits for the year-end book close**: in the **commercial report** the cost
**must be recognized**, but for **tax** it **must be corrected**. This is where commercial profit is
adjusted into taxable income.

*Note: this page is a **principle & approach** (from an accountant's practice), not a substitute for
official rules. For specific cases, always refer to the tax rules in force at the time of the
transaction.*
