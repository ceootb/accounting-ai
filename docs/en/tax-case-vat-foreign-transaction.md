# Tax Case Study: VAT on Foreign Transactions (Overseas Principal)

> **Store as a Practical Tax Judgment Case — NOT an automatic tax rule.** The VAT treatment of
> cross-border transactions **must not** be concluded from the keyword *"payment from an overseas
> principal"* alone.

## Tax knowledge ≠ automatic accounting logic

In the normal accounting workflow, a transaction is recorded **by substance & accounting treatment** →
producing the commercial report. The fiscal correction is done in the *tax reconciliation/working paper*.

> **Fiscal correction = tax knowledge / tax reconciliation knowledge**, **not** a *daily accounting
> journal rule*. Don't create an automatic journal just because an expense has a **potential** fiscal
> correction.

## Tax knowledge must be fact-based

Tax rules **cannot always** be turned into a simple rule based on the **transaction name**. In practice,
the tax treatment depends on: **substance, contract, SLA, place of supply, location of the tax object, who
performs/receives the supply, when, the type of tax object, supporting documents, the rules in force in
that period, and their interpretation**.

> Use the approach: **FACTS → CONTRACT/SLA → TAX OBJECT → PLACE OF SUPPLY/DELIVERY → APPLICABLE REGULATION
> → DOCUMENTATION → TAX CONCLUSION** — **not** *KEYWORD → AUTOMATIC TAX CONCLUSION*.

## Practical case (an accountant's experience)

There was once a **request/opinion from the tax authority** that the company charge **VAT on all income**
received from an **overseas principal**. The team **did not accept** that conclusion outright. After
examining the **transactions, contract, and SLA**, they found that for the disputed transactions, the
**handover of the tax object occurred outside Indonesia** per the SLA's delivery terms.

By understanding the **substance & contractual basis**, the company could argue that the transaction is
**not automatically** subject to VAT **merely because** *"the payment comes from an overseas principal."*
The analysis must look at **what the transaction's object is** and **where the relevant supply/event
occurs** for tax, based on facts & applicable rules.

> **A different condition:** if the object is **shipped from Indonesia** but **never reaches** the location
> agreed in the contract/SLA — so the company **receives no payment** for that delivery — then the
> transaction facts **must be analyzed differently**.

## Don't make simplistic rules

Don't say: *"Overseas principal → automatically **not** a VAT object"*, nor *"Payment from abroad →
automatically VAT-able."* **Both are too simplistic.** First request & analyze:

1. Who is the **principal/customer**? 2. What is the **transaction object**? 3. What **goods/services** are
actually provided? 4. What's in the **contract**? 5. What's in the **SLA**? 6. **Delivery/supply location**
per the contract? 7. Where does the supply **factually occur**? 8. Did the **delivery actually happen**?
9. Did the company **receive payment**? 10. **Supporting documents**? 11. **VAT rules** in force in the
transaction period? 12. Any **special treatment** for that transaction type?

## Tax judgment & human review

A finance/accounting PIC must be able to **read the rules, check facts, read the contract/SLA, gather
evidence, and present an argument on a legal basis** when interpretation differs from the authority.
**However**, the system **must NOT** teach: **hiding facts; manipulating contracts; making documents look
like a different transaction; illegally avoiding tax; or arguments not based on facts.**

> The argument must **always** rest on: **THE ACTUAL FACTS + DOCUMENTS + CONTRACT/SLA + THE APPLICABLE
> RULES.**
>
> **Principle:** *Tax planning = legal compliance + understanding the rules + understanding the facts +
> proper documentation + ability to explain/argue the position.* **Not** finding ways not to pay tax.

## Architecture principle — four layers (don't mix)

| Layer | Role |
|---|---|
| **Accounting Engine** | records transactions by **accounting substance** |
| **Tax Knowledge** | stores **rules, concepts, & case studies** (with *effective date/version*) |
| **Tax Reconciliation** | *adjusts* from **commercial profit → fiscal profit** |
| **Tax Judgment** | **ambiguous** areas needing analysis of facts/documents/contract/SLA + **human review** |

> **Don't mix** the four layers into one *automatic journal logic*. If the tax treatment is ambiguous:
> **DO NOT ASSUME → ASK FOR FACTS → CHECK CONTRACT/SLA → CHECK APPLICABLE RULE → CHECK SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS
> → FLAG FOR HUMAN REVIEW.**
