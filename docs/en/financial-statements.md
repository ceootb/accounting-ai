# Financial Statements & the Debit-Credit Rule (Foundation)

> The **master framework** for understanding accounting. Once you grasp these three statements +
> **normal balances**, the debit/credit side of every account becomes automatic — and that is the
> basis of **auto-journaling**. *(Initial draft — curated by a practicing accountant; open to refinement.)*

---

## 1. The Accounting Equation (the "balance sheet must balance" analogy)

```
ASSETS = LIABILITIES + EQUITY
```
Extended with current-period activity:
```
ASSETS = LIABILITIES + EQUITY + (REVENUE − EXPENSES)
```

**Key analogy:** the Balance Sheet must **BALANCE — left side (Assets) = right side (Liabilities +
Equity)**. Because every transaction is recorded **double-entry (Debit = Credit)**, **all reports
always balance total debit vs total credit**. If it doesn't balance → some journal is wrong/unbalanced.

---

## 2. Normal Balances — CORE MEMORIZATION

| Account Group | Normal Balance | Increases on | Decreases on | Appears in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Assets** | **Debit** | Debit | Credit | Balance Sheet |
| **Expenses** | **Debit** | Debit | Credit | Income Statement |
| **Liabilities** | **Credit** | Credit | Debit | Balance Sheet |
| **Equity** | **Credit** | Credit | Debit | Balance Sheet / Changes in Equity |
| **Revenue** | **Credit** | Credit | Debit | Income Statement |

**Memory trick:** **Assets & Expenses → Debit**; **Liabilities, Equity, Revenue → Credit.**
Principle: **the normal-balance side is the side that increases the account** (the opposite decreases it).

*(Contra accounts oppose their parent — e.g. Accumulated Depreciation [contra-asset] & Allowance for
Doubtful Accounts have a Credit normal balance; Drawings/Dividends [contra-equity] & Sales Returns/
Discounts [contra-revenue] have a Debit normal balance.)*

---

## 3. The Three Main Statements & How They Connect

### a. Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position)
A snapshot **at a single date**. `Assets = Liabilities + Equity` → **must balance**.

### b. Income Statement (P&L)
Performance **over a period**. `Revenue − Expenses = Net Profit/Loss`.

### c. Statement of Changes in Equity
The bridge from P&L to the Balance Sheet:
```
Beginning Equity + Net Profit (from P&L) − Drawings/Dividends = Ending Equity
```

**How they link (why everything stays balanced):**
```
Net Profit (Income Statement)  →  Changes in Equity  →  Ending Equity  →  Balance Sheet
```
Net profit flows into equity via the Changes in Equity statement — so the Balance Sheet stays balanced.
The three statements are **one connected set**, not separate.

---

## 4. Why Reports Always Balance

Every journal: **total Debit = total Credit**. Accumulating all journals → the **Trial Balance**
balances → the Balance Sheet balances. If it **doesn't balance**, some journal is unbalanced/mis-posted
and must be traced. This is the built-in "alarm" of double-entry.

---

## 5. Link to Auto-Journaling (next chapter)

Because **each account already has a fixed normal balance & target statement**, the system can produce
**auto-journals**: once the transaction type is known (e.g. Sales Invoice / Purchase Invoice), the Debit
& Credit accounts are **predetermined**, and the result automatically "lands" on the correct report line.
Junior staff only enter a voucher; the system keeps debit-credit balanced.
