# Tax Case Study: Operational Car Taken Home by a Director/Manager

> **Store as Tax Knowledge / Case Study — NOT an automatic accounting rule.** A vehicle's fiscal
> treatment **must not** be decided from the keyword *"car taken home by a director/manager"* alone.

## 1. Regulatory basis — the 50% rule (historical)

**KEP-220/PJ./2002** (income-tax treatment of cellphone & company-vehicle costs). A **sedan or similar**
owned and used by the company for **certain employees due to their position/work** has a limitation:
- **depreciation/acquisition & major repair** cost → **50% deductible**;
- **routine maintenance/repair** cost → **50% deductible**.

> Don't over-extend it to *"any company car taken home is only 50% deductible."* The analysis must check
> whether the vehicle **falls within the condition** referred to.

## 2. Development — PMK 66/2023

**PMK 66/2023** (income tax on **benefits-in-kind / enjoyment (natura/kenikmatan)**), **effective 1 July
2023**, revoking **PMK 167/PMK.03/2018**. In general, benefit-in-kind costs **can be deductible** from the
employer's gross income **as long as** they are costs to **earn, collect, and maintain** income. Enjoyment
with a **useful life > 1 year** is expensed via **depreciation/amortization**.

The official DGT FAQ gives an example: a vehicle facility for **directors** (e.g. a sedan) **may be 100%
depreciated as long as** the sedan is **provided as employment compensation** (a vehicle facility for the
employee).

> Don't turn this into a rule *"a director's car = automatically 100%."* Still look at the **substance,
> purpose, status, use, and facts** of the transaction.

## 3. Practical case (an accountant's experience)

A company has an **operational car** for operational activities. It **may be taken home** by a
director/manager, but its **main function remains an operational vehicle** — not purely a personal car.
During an audit, the **tax authority may question** the vehicle's depreciation/cost because it is taken
home by an officer. But with an **argument based on the substance & operational function**, **no fiscal
correction** was made to that vehicle cost.

> Store as a **PRACTICAL TAX CASE / TAX JUDGMENT CASE**, not an automatic rule. **Practical note:** the
> field outcome can depend on the **strength of the substance-based argument & documentation** from the
> company's PIC when facing the tax examiner.

## 4. Questions to ask before concluding

For a company car taken home by a director/manager, **ask first**:
1. Is it a **company asset**? 2. Used for **operational activities**? 3. Given as a **benefit-in-kind** to
the employee? 4. Given **because of position/work**? 5. Does its use **relate to the job**? 6. Can it be
used for **personal** purposes? 7. Is there a **policy/SOP** on vehicle use? 8. Is there **evidence** of
operational use? 9. **Who bears** fuel, maintenance, repair, vehicle tax, etc.? 10. How is it recorded as a
**fixed asset**? 11. How is **commercial depreciation** recorded? 12. Which **tax year/period**? 13. Which
**tax rules** applied in that period? 14. Any relevant special rule (KEP-220/PJ./2002 or PMK 66/2023)?

## 5. Don't oversimplify

Don't use logic like *taken home → 50%*, or *director → 100%*, or *operational car → automatically 100%*.
Use the flow:

> **FACTS → SUBSTANCE → APPLICABLE REGULATION → DOCUMENTATION → TAX TREATMENT.**

If there's a difference between the historical rule and the rules after PMK 66/2023, **state the
period/tax year first** before concluding.

## 6. Distinguish three things

- **A. Accounting treatment** — commercial depreciation follows accounting policy & the asset's substance.
- **B. Fiscal treatment** — deductible/non-deductible follows the applicable tax rules.
- **C. Tax reconciliation** — if commercial depreciation ≠ the fiscally deductible amount, the difference
  goes into **fiscal reconciliation**.

> **Don't change the commercial depreciation journal** just because of a fiscal limitation.

## 7. Principle

Tax treatment can't always be decided from an **account name** or a **single fact** (e.g. "taken home").
The substance of use + a fact-based argument can be **very important**. If the rules still need
interpretation or there may be a difference between the taxpayer and the authority:

> **DO NOT ASSUME → identify the facts → check the applicable regulation → check the period → check
> documentation → FLAG FOR TAX REVIEW.**

Store the **experience** as a *practical case study*; store the **regulations** as *regulatory knowledge*
with an **effective date/version**. **Don't** turn one company's experience into a **universal tax rule**.
